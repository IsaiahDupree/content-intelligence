## Transcript

Your AI agent does not need more freedom. It needs a clear way to stop. Teams connect agents to email, customer data, or production tools, then judge them by a successful demo. But the real test is a vague instruction, bad data, or an action that costs money. So before you add another ability, give the workflow three simple controls. First, define what it may read, what it may change, and how much it may spend. Next, require a person to approve actions that reach a customer, move money, or delete data. Then, keep a receipt showing the input, decision, action, result, and person who approved it. For example, run one bad-input test. If the agent pauses, explains the problem, and leaves a useful receipt, your controls are working. A trustworthy agent is not one that never fails. It is one that fails visibly and can be stopped. Book an AI Automation Audit to map one risky workflow before you use it with write access.
